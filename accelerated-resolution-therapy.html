Sometimes you can understand exactly what happened and still feel as though your body never got the message that it is over.

A memory, image, sound, conversation, or situation can bring back fear, shame, anger, grief, or a sense that you are right back in the experience. You may know you are safe in the present while your nervous system responds as if the past is happening again.

Insight matters. But insight alone doesn't always change the way a painful memory lives in the nervous system.

What is Accelerated Resolution Therapy?

Accelerated Resolution Therapy, commonly called ART, is a structured form of psychotherapy that uses guided imagery and sets of side-to-side eye movements. The process is designed to help you work with distressing memories, images, emotions, and physical sensations in a focused way.

You remain awake, aware, and in control throughout the process. ART is not hypnosis, and you do not have to describe every detail of a difficult experience out loud. I guide the structure of the session while you decide what you want to share.

Changing the response, not the history

During ART, you bring a memory or concern to mind and notice the images, feelings, and body sensations connected to it. Guided eye movements are used while you work with that material. A technique called voluntary image replacement then gives you an opportunity to reshape distressing imagery in a way that feels meaningful to you.

The goal is not to erase the memory, pretend it did not happen, or replace your history with a different story. The goal is to help reduce the emotional and physical intensity attached to the memory so that it has less power over the present.

What happened can remain part of your story without continuing to feel like it is happening now.

ART may be considered for

  • Trauma and PTSD symptoms
  • Distressing memories or intrusive images
  • Anxiety, panic, or phobias
  • Grief and loss
  • Shame, guilt, or poor self-image
  • Performance anxiety or job-related stress
  • Painful relationship experiences

ART has been studied as a focused treatment for trauma-related symptoms. Early research is promising, and the evidence base is still developing. Some people notice meaningful change quickly, while others need more time or a different approach. No therapy can promise a particular result or timeline.

We will decide together

Before beginning ART, we can talk about what you are experiencing, what you hope will change, and whether this kind of structured memory work feels appropriate for you. ART may be used for a specific concern or integrated into a broader course of trauma-informed therapy.

You do not have to force the process or tell your whole story at once. We can begin with safety, move at a manageable pace, and make room for questions along the way.
