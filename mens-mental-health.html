Suck it up. Don't cry. Keep moving. Don't show weakness.

Many men grow up learning that emotions are something to control, hide, or push through rather than something to understand. Over time, that can make it difficult to recognize what you're feeling, talk openly about what's happening, or ask for help before things reach a breaking point.

You may have learned to cope by staying busy, working harder, exercising, isolating yourself, drinking, using substances, distracting yourself, or simply telling yourself that other people have it worse.

From the outside, you may look like you're doing fine

Internally, you might feel anxious, angry, exhausted, disconnected, overwhelmed, lonely, or completely stuck.

You might also find yourself struggling with relationships, intimacy, fatherhood, work, identity, or the feeling that you're somehow failing despite doing everything you're “supposed” to do.

You don't have to suffer in silence.

Therapy can be practical and direct

Therapy doesn't require you to become someone you're not. You don't have to sit on a couch and talk about your feelings for an hour if that isn't how you operate. Therapy can be practical, direct, collaborative, and focused on understanding what's actually happening beneath the surface.

At Root & River Counseling in Boise, I work with men navigating anxiety, depression, trauma, anger, relationship difficulties, life transitions, emotional disconnection, and men's mental health concerns.

I believe it's okay to have feelings. It's okay to be vulnerable. And it's okay to ask for help.

Therapy can be a place where you don't have to perform, hold everything together, or pretend you're okay.

No performance required
